> **At a glance** - Scorecards measure sales reps' performance against set KPIs, allowing you to establish targets and scoring mechanisms.

Scorecards are a great way to measure your sales reps' performance against the KPIs that are set. After [Step 1 - Define KPIs](/modules/kpi-gamification/features/define-kpis) and [Step 2 - Create Field Mappings](/modules/kpi-gamification/create-and-manage/how-to-create-field-mapping), this is the third step in the KPI Gamification process setup.

Scorecards allow you to measure these KPIs daily, weekly, monthly, quarterly, or yearly. You can set targets for teams and also establish individual targets for new joiners or experienced reps. This is also where you determine the scoring mechanism. Before you start creating a scorecard, consider these two important aspects:

## When to use this
- To measure KPIs effectively, aligning with your organization's goals.
- When you need to set realistic targets for sales reps.
- To create a balanced scoring system that reflects the importance of each KPI.

## Before you start
Consider the following aspects to ensure effective scorecard creation:
- Your organization needs to have defined KPIs.
- You should have established field mappings.

## Steps

### 1. Measurement Duration and Target
Determine the best duration for measuring each KPI. Some KPIs are best measured daily, while others are more suitable for quarterly assessment. It depends on the KPI and its historical achievement. For example, a sales rep might be able to close the sale of a maximum of four capital equipment items in a month. If measuring the KPI called 'Number of Capital Equipments Sold', the best measurement duration will be monthly. However, if one of your organization's goals is to distribute sales evenly throughout the month, it may make sense to measure it weekly and set a target of one per week. Ensure that the targets are realistic to motivate reps when they achieve them.

### 2. Scoring
Scores determine the points a participant receives when achieving certain KPIs. You will almost always start with a couple of KPIs for a game. Each KPI can have a score associated with it, and the scores should be well-balanced. The number of points assigned to each KPI should ideally reflect its importance.
